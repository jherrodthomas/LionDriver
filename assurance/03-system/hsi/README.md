# Hardware-software interface

The hardware-software interface (HSI), [WP-S-05](../WP-S-05-hsi-specification.md), is where the safety argument meets
the circuit board. It lists every link of the panda safety MCU: to the comma device's main processor (the SoC), to the
car's CAN buses, to the harness relay and to its own watchdog, flash and fault monitors. For each link it gives what
crosses it, what protects it, and what the safety requirements still need from it. All facts are taken from the
firmware at the baseline; the pins and electrical values still have to be confirmed against the schematic.

This page walks through it with its figures. The document is the source of record, and every figure here is generated
from its tables.

**The HSI in five lines.**

1. **Fourteen interfaces, one safety MCU.** Twelve are ASIL C, and harness orientation and ignition sensing are ASIL
   B. One, memory protection and RAM error correction (HSI-14), does not exist in the firmware yet.
2. **The SoC link is weakly protected.** Every transfer carries an 8-bit XOR and nothing else: no CRC with a known
   residual error, no sequence counter, no freshness (TSR-410, 411; OI-2).
3. **The SoC can ask for too much.** Of the 28 control requests, 9 that must be refused or limited in a car mode have
   no gate at the baseline. Among them are driving the relay, the softloader and setting the safety mode. The
   reference build locks the safety mode (TSR-512); the rest wait for the release whitelist (OI-3).
4. **The SoC can replace the MCU firmware** through the boot and reset lines (HSI-08). Read and write protection of the
   flash is required and not in place (TSR-511, GAP-38).
5. **End-to-end protection is thin on the reference car.** The vehicle signals the envelope gates on carry at most a
   checksum and no counter. So the argument rests on timeouts, plausibility and the envelope's own content checks,
   not on end-to-end protection (§12).

## 1. The interfaces

![WP-S-05 Figure 2-1: the safety MCU with its 14 hardware-software interfaces: four to the SoC, five inside the MCU, and the vehicle CAN buses, the harness and the shared speaker on the other side; 12 ASIL C, 2 ASIL B](../figures/fig-s05-2-1-hsi-overview.svg)

*The fourteen interfaces (§2, Figure 2-1).* Left, the links to the SoC: the SPI link (HSI-01), the control requests
and CAN packets that travel on it (HSI-02, -03), and the SoC's reset and boot lines (HSI-08). Middle, what is inside
the MCU: watchdog, fault monitors, flash and bootstub, supply measurement, and the memory protection still to come.
Right, the car side: the CAN buses (HSI-04), the harness relay, orientation and ignition (HSI-05…07), and the speaker
it shares with the SoC (HSI-11).

## 2. The SoC link

Every SPI transfer is a 7-byte header, an acknowledge, the data and a response, each closed by an 8-bit XOR (§4.1).
CAN frames for the car ride inside it as packets with their own XOR (§4.3).

| What the link has | What it lacks | Required |
|---|---|---|
| XOR checksum per header, per payload and per CAN packet | a CRC with a known residual error; an XOR misses many even-weight and burst errors | TSR-410; the target is < 10⁻⁹ undetected corrupted actuation transfers per hour (OI-2) |
| ACK / NACK; the host retries | a sequence counter and freshness on the command endpoint | TSR-411 |
| A 4096-byte buffer on the MCU | bounds checks on every length (NF-04); the host uses 2048 bytes | TSR-412; OI-5 |

## 3. What the SoC can ask for

![WP-S-05 Figure 4-1: the 28 control requests of §4.2 grouped by the treatment a release build needs in a car mode: refused, limited, allowed or not handled; each with its function, its effect and its gating at the baseline, and 9 that must be refused or limited marked as having no gate today](../figures/fig-s05-4-1-control-requests.svg)

*The control requests (§4.2, Figure 4-1).* The SoC configures the MCU through control requests on endpoint 0. Most
of them only read something. The ones that matter can release the relay, replace the firmware, reroute or slow down
a CAN bus, switch the transceivers off, or set the safety mode. The tiles with a red border have no gate at the
baseline, so the SoC can use them in a car mode today. That is the freedom-from-interference claim FFI-2 of the TSC
(reconfiguring the safety element). The reference build locks the safety mode and parameter (TSR-512); the
whitelist for everything else per mode is OI-3.

## 4. The CAN buses and the signals the envelope uses

The MCU has three CAN controllers (§5). Bus 0 is the car side of the harness, bus 2 the camera side, and bus 1 the
diagnostic bus used for fingerprinting. The envelope gates on six messages from the car:

| Message | Rate | Checksum | Counter | What the envelope takes from it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `0xAA` wheel speeds | 83 Hz | no | no | vehicle moving, speed |
| `0x260` steering torque | 50 Hz | yes | no | EPS torque for tracking, driver torque |
| `0x1D2` PCM cruise | 33 Hz | yes | no | the engagement edge, gas |
| `0x226` brake module | 40 Hz | no | no | brake |
| `0x262`, `0x1D3` EPS status, PCM cruise 2 | to be measured (OI-7) | yes | no | presence, checksum and timeout in the reference build; their content is not used yet (TSR-110, 405) |

None of them has a counter, and two have no checksum at all (GAP-01). The 29 safety-relevant signals that cross the
interfaces are catalogued one per row in §11, each with its owner, integrity, liveness, maximum age, failure reaction,
TSR and the integration test that covers it.

![SysML view V-19: the 29 signals of WP-S-05 §11 by interface, with their owner elements, whether the signal carries a checksum and a liveness indication of its own, the ASIL, the TSRs it serves and its test cases](../../models/sysml/generated/fig-sysml-v19-hsi-signals.svg)

*The signals in the system model (SysML view V-19).* Every signal of §11 is an item in the SysML model, owned by
elements of the architecture and tied to its TSRs and test cases; CI fails if one of them does not exist (check H3).
Eleven signals carry no checksum, CRC or XOR of their own, and 22 no counter, alive check or supervision. For those,
the receiver's own checks are the protection: rates, timeouts and cross-checks.

![SysML view V-20: the 14 interfaces of WP-S-05 §11.4, one row each, with what the hardware provides and what the software must do, their owner elements, how a failure of the other side is detected, the reaction and the status: one in place, twelve partial, one missing](../../models/sysml/generated/fig-sysml-v20-hsi-responsibilities.svg)

*Who does what at each interface (SysML view V-20).* Where hardware and software share a safety function, WP-S-05 §11.4
says what each side provides and must do, who detects a failure of the other and how the item reacts. Each row is an
item in the SysML model, with a hardware owner and a software owner that must be elements of the right kind (check H4).
Only the watchdog is in place. At three interfaces a failure of the other side is not detected today: the relay has no
readback, the ignition line no plausibility check, and the siren no feedback. The memory protection and RAM error
correction of HSI-14 do not exist yet.

## 5. Relay, reset, boot and watchdog

- **Relay (HSI-05).** Open-drain and active-low, so a released or reset MCU releases the relay and restores the stock
  camera path. It is driven only in a car mode with a harness detected. There is no read-back of the relay contact
  (GAP-12, GAP-43; TSR-506).
- **Reset and boot (HSI-08).** The SoC drives the MCU's reset and boot-mode lines. With boot mode high it reaches the
  ST ROM bootloader, from where the flash can be rewritten. Read and write protection is required (TSR-511) and not
  in place.
- **Bootstub (HSI-12).** It checks the application's signature (SHA-1 and RSA-1024) against the release key, and a
  debug key in debug builds. A modern signature and no debug key are required (TSR-514).
- **Watchdog (HSI-09).** The independent watchdog runs in window mode with a 90 ms nominal timeout once the relay is
  first energised (TSR-501). Fault flags are reported but do not yet lead to the safe state (TSR-502).

## 6. Memory and modes

The SPI DMA buffers sit in SRAM1/2 and are written by the SoC; the safety state (`controls_allowed`, limits, samples)
sits in DTCM. Nothing yet stops a DMA overrun from reaching it: a memory-protection boundary and RAM error correction
are required (§8; TSR-503, 507; OI-4). The MCU's modes and power states are in §9, and the state machines that move
between them are drawn in the [TSC](../tsc/README.md) (WP-S-03 Figure 4-5).

## 7. Vehicle networks and end-to-end protection

§12 asks what protects the information end to end, hop by hop. A CAN controller's CRC protects one hop only: every
controller recomputes it on sending, so a frame corrupted inside the panda or the SoC leaves with a valid CRC. On the
reference car, full end-to-end protection (checksum, counter and timeout together) exists only on the camera's
pre-collision frames, which the item forwards untouched. The commands the item sends are packed by the QM SoC, so no
end-to-end check can be credited against SoC faults. The argument therefore rests on timeouts and plausibility on the
receive side (TSR-401…405, mostly not built), the envelope's content checks on the transmit side, and a stronger SPI
link (TSR-410, 411). The [CAN, CAN FD and end-to-end protection](../../../wiki/CAN-CAN-FD-and-end-to-end-protection.md)
wiki page draws the hops as animated figures.

## 8. Open items

The ones that can change the interface are below; the full list is in WP-S-05 §10.

- **OI-1:** confirm pins, the relay driver, transceiver pull-ups and GPIO reset behaviour against the schematic.
- **OI-2:** set the SPI residual-error target, choose the CRC and specify the sequence counter.
- **OI-3:** define the release whitelist of control requests per mode.
- **OI-4:** define the memory-protection regions so the SPI DMA buffers cannot reach the safety state.
- **OI-9, OI-10:** write the EPS's own checks of the steering frame as an assumption of use, and decide whether the
  envelope verifies the Toyota checksum before sending.
