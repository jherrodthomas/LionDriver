- **Ideal steering**: it knows the gust's push exactly and adds it at once, on top of the plant's law; only the torque limiter and the EPS lag hold it back. A bound on what any lane keeping can do with this steering.

## What this shows (generated)

- **Gusts that come and go** (0.5–8 s): with ideal steering the car moves at most 26 cm, 27 % of the room in a 3.7 m lane. The steering's rise rate and lag are fast enough for these.
- **A step** (leaving a cutting or a bridge into the full wind): 18 cm at the lower load band, but **241 cm at the upper band**. There the steady push alone (1.76 m/s²) takes 89 % of the steering's 1.99 m/s², so little is left to bring the car back; even ideal steering leaves the lane.
- So the question for the windy-day log (WP-C-02 OI-10) is not only how hard a 30 km/h wind pushes but how much steering is left: near the upper band the Corolla's torque authority, not the lane keeping, decides. This adds to DQ-415's finding that the curve margin does not cover crosswind.
- The plant's stand-in moves further on long gusts because it has no integral action; openpilot plans its path back to the lane centre, so the ideal line is the better guide.
