# Steering authority for the ODD's curves

> **Synthetic data (DSR-12). Design aid, no evidence credit (DQ-413).** Generated by [`weather/steer_authority.py`](../weather/steer_authority.py) from openpilot's torque controller and opendbc's torque data, read and pinned; do not edit by hand. The torque data is comma's fit on fleet data, not a measurement of the reference car (WP-C-02 OI-2 asks for that).

openpilot's torque controller limits its output to the lateral acceleration full torque gives (`latcontrol_torque.py`: `lateral_accel_from_torque(steer_max)`). For the Corolla TSS2 that is **1.99 m/s²** (`LAT_ACCEL_FACTOR`); the most comma measured is 1.87 m/s². The online learner may move the factor ±30 % (1.39–2.59). The ODD's curve bound asks for 2.5 m/s² (R = v² / 2.5); WP-C-02 takes the controller's limit as 3.0 m/s² (`drive_helpers.py`), the curvature the planner may ask for, not what the torque can deliver.

## What this shows (generated)

- **On a flat curve at the ODD's bound the steering cannot hold the lane.** The bound asks for 2.5 m/s²; the controller's ceiling is 1.99 (1.87 measured), -0.51 m/s² short. At 110 km/h the tightest flat curve it holds is 469 m, against R_ODD's 374 m.
- **A banked curve closes the gap, only just.** At 6 % superelevation the road gives 0.59 m/s² and the steering needs 1.91: 0.08 m/s² to spare. An adverse crown makes it -0.70.
- **Wind takes the rest.** A 30 km/h side wind adds the crosswind study's 0.9–1.8 m/s²: on any of these curves the total is over the ceiling.
- **What happens then:** the controller saturates and the car runs wide of the lane; openpilot raises `steerSaturated` after 0.4 s (WP-S-04 §3.8). WP-C-02 §3.2 already rests the curve bound on AOU-01 (EPS authority suffices), not characterised (GAP-04, OI-2). The fleet fit says that assumption does not hold at 2.5 m/s² on a flat curve. **A question for the lead** (an ODD limit), queued as DQ-444.
