## Braking downhill

## The low beam over a sag curve

AASHTO builds a sag so a beam diverging 1° upward from headlights 0.6 m up meets the road at the stopping sight distance. Our car is at the sag's low point, the road rising ahead. A low beam's cut-off points 0.57° down; on a flat road it meets the road 65 m ahead (the night-car study).

## What this shows (generated)

- **On the ODD's 6 % downhill the item's −3.5 m/s² needs a friction of 0.42**, against 0.36 on the level. Dry and wet with new tyres there is room (+0.16 wet). With the ODD's 3 mm of tread the wet margin is +0.12 (Continental) to +0.01 (MIRA); almost nothing left at MIRA's end.
- **A driver's full stop grows downhill**: wet with 3 mm (MIRA) from 188 m on the level to 207 m on 6 %.
- **openpilot does not plan for the grade**: the planner's −3.5 m/s² is the car's own deceleration and the PCM closes the loop on it; the pitch terms only shape transients and the brake permit. So the braking the item can count on is the friction left after gravity, as above.
- **Over a sag built for 110 km/h the low beam's bright part meets the road 46 m ahead**, against 65 m on the flat and the 220 m AASHTO's 1° beam assumes. AASHTO's beam is the top edge of the light, which in a modern low beam is the dim part above the cut-off; the item's 149 m stop is far beyond either. A sag at night is the night-car study's case, a little worse.
- For WP-C-02 OI-5: the grade bound costs none of the item's braking inside the ODD's tyre and rain bounds, but leaves almost no margin at MIRA's end of 3 mm wet: a line for OI-5's rationale, not a register question.
