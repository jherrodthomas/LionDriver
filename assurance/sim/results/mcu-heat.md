## A trip point for TSR-509

The DTS may read up to 7 K low (+1 K for the supply) and 2 K high (+1 K). A trip point must sit low enough to act before the junction leaves its range, and high enough not to trip on the hottest drive inside the ODD (the hot July day with the AC off: MCU block 68 °C, [`cabin-device.md`](cabin-device.md)).

## What this shows (generated)

- At 35 °C cabin air the MCU block runs at 63 °C; about 12 K of it is the SoC's.
- When the SoC reaches its limit, the MCU's junction is at 79–90 °C as built and 86–97 °C with the fan stalled: below the industrial grade's 125 °C. While the host runs its thermal policy, the SoC limit is reached first and the MCU is still in range. Without it (a hung host still drawing power), the MCU would leave its range only at about 85 °C cabin air as built, or 61 °C with the fan stalled, close to the 62 °C the mount air reaches parked on a hot July day ([`cabin-soak.md`](cabin-soak.md)). Two faults together, but the case TSR-509 is for.
- That protection depends on the host. TSR-509 asks the MCU to act on its own DTS, and the numbers leave room for it: a trip at a reading of about 117 °C or below (industrial part) keeps 35 K from the hottest drive in the ODD. A candidate for the HWSR-509 owner; the part's suffix and package (T1) and the device's own logs of `temperature_pkt` settle it.
