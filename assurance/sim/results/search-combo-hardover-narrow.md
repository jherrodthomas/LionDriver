A hard-over within the envelope, to the right or mirrored to the left, on lanes of 3.0–3.3 m (TC-32: a narrow lane, often beside a truck or a barrier), on a straight or a gentle left curve. In a 3.0 m lane the body edge is 0.61 m from the line. The adjacent truck or barrier is not modelled; the time to the line says how soon it would be reached. A steady crosswind or bank of up to 0.5 m/s² either way (TC-15) is held by the item with a small offset before the fault, which eats into the margin on one side.

Faults: F-01b hard-over (right), F-01b mirrored (left). Robustness: rho = (lane width − 1.781 m) / 2 − max |lateral offset| after the fault; rho < 0 means the body edge crossed a lane line. Beyond about −2 m the car is in the next lane and the magnitude means little; the sign and the time to the line are what count.

What the plant cannot show: it has **no tyre friction limit** (a linear tyre: a wet or icy road, a braking driver in a curve and a correction of 4 m/s² all behave as on a dry road, so nothing here says whether the car would slide) and **no perception** (the lane-keeping controller sees its lane exactly, so no perception insufficiency, wrong line or late detection is searched; the faults are injected at the actuator side). The driver is a model with stated reactions, the EPS response to each fault is assumed (M-01, M-02), and road users beside the ego are not modelled: the time to the lane line is how soon an adjacent vehicle or barrier would be reached, not whether it is hit.
