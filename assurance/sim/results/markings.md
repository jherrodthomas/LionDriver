# Lane markings at night in rain: the contrast the camera gets

> **Synthetic data (DSR-12). Design aid, no evidence credit (DQ-413).** Generated by [`weather/markings.py`](../weather/markings.py) from NCHRP Project 20-102(06) and the headlight study's veil ([`headlights.md`](headlights.md)); do not edit by hand. The thresholds are a Mobileye camera's on a closed course, not openpilot's model.

Under the car's own low beams the line-to-road contrast ratio is the ratio of their retroreflectivities. The pavement returns 13.6 mcd/m²/lx dry and 2 wet. A machine-vision system detected every marking above a ratio of 2.5 at night dry, 3.0 with oncoming glare and 2.1 wet (NCHRP 20-102(06)).

## What this shows (generated)

- **Dry, no glare:** a line at the US minimum of 50 mcd/m²/lx gives a ratio of 3.7, above the 2.5 the camera needed; the minimum is enough.
- **Dry, oncoming glare:** low beams across a median barely move it (3.6). An undipped high beam across an 8 m median drops it to 2.2 with 3 % scatter and 1.5 with 10 %; a line then needs 73–149 mcd/m²/lx, more than the US minimum.
- **Wet:** the road returns so little light that a wet line needs only 4 mcd/m²/lx without glare. But the same glare weighs about 7 times more over a wet road: with an undipped high beam across the median a wet line needs 22–64 mcd/m²/lx, and even a good marking's 35 gives 2.8–1.6.
- The MUTCD sets no wet level, so a road can meet the US minimum and still lose its lines to the camera in rain with oncoming high beams.
- For KS-03 and KS-15: test worn lines at night in rain, with an oncoming car on high beam across the median. The ODD's light-rain bound (WP-C-02) keeps rain in.
