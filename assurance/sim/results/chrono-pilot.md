**What this shows** (generated from the rows above):

- Curve entry: the times agree to within 0.04 s (driver cue and 3.0 m lane line). There the drift is the geometry of a car that stops turning, and the L1 plant gets it right; the peak drift after the correction differs, but by then the car is in the next lane and the magnitude means little.
- Straight with a cross slope: the Chrono vehicle drifts more slowly; the driver notices later in 6 of 6 runs with 0.2 m/s² or more. A real slope is resisted by the tyres' slip, which the L1 push, added as a lateral acceleration, is not; the L1 plant is the conservative one here. The Chrono item also holds the lane centre against the slope (an integral term), where the L1 item stands off it, so the time to the line starts from a different offset.
- With no slope at all the Chrono vehicle still drifts 0.35–0.39 m in 8 s from the small heading the item leaves at the loss: the noise floor of this pilot.
- The aligning torque and the hands on the wheel (the L1 caveat of straight-drift.md) stay open: Chrono's standard steering cannot show them. The L2 Modelica column (sim README §3.5) is the place for the steering side; the vehicle side is what Chrono adds, and it would need the reference Corolla's tyre and suspension parameters (WP-S-04 M-02) before it says more than a generic sedan does.
