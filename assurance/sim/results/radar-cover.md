At 76.5 GHz liquid water has a permittivity of 6.9 − j9.9 near freezing and 8.8 − j16.0 at 20 °C: inside it the wave loses about 22 dB per millimetre each way. Ice (3.15) hardly absorbs. The radar's signal crosses the layer twice; its range falls to the share of the amplitude that gets through.

## What this shows (generated)

- **A rain film costs range fast.** A tenth of a millimetre of water takes 10 dB both ways and leaves 55 % of the range; half a millimetre 28 dB and 20 %. At a tenth of a millimetre absorption is 4 dB of it; the rest is the film's reflection, which a thin layer of so high a permittivity already has.
- **Clear ice is nearly transparent.** 5 mm of it costs 2.8 dB (85 % of the range); its reflections, not its loss, set that.
- **Slush is between, and the water in it decides.** Wet slush 2 mm thick takes 48 dB (6 %).
- **What depends on the radar:** at highway speed openpilot takes a lead only from the driving model and uses the radar to confirm it (the stopped-car study), so a wet cover weakens the radar's part, not the lead. The stock PCS is different: it is the backstop the night, curve and stopped-car studies leave to the car, and it brakes on this radar. A wet or slushy emblem shortens its reach, and this study cannot say when the car notices.
- For KS-15 and the owner's information: test the PCS backstop in rain with spray; tell the driver that slush on the emblem can blind the radar the car's emergency braking relies on. The cover's material (T1) moves the thin-film numbers, not the trend.
