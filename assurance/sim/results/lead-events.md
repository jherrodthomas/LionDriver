> **Synthetic data (DSR-12). Design aid, no evidence credit (DQ-413).** Generated by [`planner/lead_events.py`](../planner/lead_events.py) with openpilot's own longitudinal MPC (`long_mpc.py`, built by [`mpc_build.py`](../planner/mpc_build.py)) in a closed loop with a point-mass car; do not edit by hand. The leads are scripted and the model's latency in taking a new lead is assumed (0.2–1 s). The stock PCS is not modelled.

The item brakes at most at 3.5 m/s² (ACCEL_MIN). The planner follows at v·T + 6 m with T = 1.25 / 1.45 / 1.75 s (aggressive / standard / relaxed). The car follows the target after 0.15 s.

## What this shows (generated)

- **A cut-in:** the planner ramps its braking up rather than braking hard at once (its jerk and acceleration-change costs); it reaches the 3.5 m/s² clip in 0 of 36 runs and hits the car in 4 (up to 27 km/h: cut-ins half a second ahead and 20–30 km/h slower).
- **A cut-out is the planner's hard case.** At 110 km/h the uncovered car is 75–151 m away and the item alone hits it in 12 of 12 runs. Impacts happen at every speed studied when the lead swerves late. The stopping is the driver's and the stock PCS's (WP-C-02 F-02).
- **A lead braking hard:** the planner takes 0.6–2.6 s after the lead starts braking to reach its 3.5 m/s² clip, and the item alone hits the lead in 23 of 24 runs, even at 4 m/s². openpilot's FCW comes 1.2–6.4 s after the lead starts braking; a driver who brakes fully 1.5 s after it still hits in 18 of 24: the stock PCS, not modelled here, has to act first.
- For KS tests: cut-outs uncovering a stopped car are where the item's braking bound stops covering the scenario; the stock PCS (not modelled) is the backstop. A drive log with the model's lead latency would turn the latency band into a line.
