# Silent assist loss on a straight: time from the drift to the lane line

> **Synthetic data (DSR-12).** Generated by [`plant/straight_drift.py`](../plant/straight_drift.py); do not edit by hand. The real panda safety code of the reference build runs in the loop with its siren muted (what-if `whatif_silent_panda`), because a 12 V supply loss of the item leaves nothing to sound it. Driver, EPS and road are the plant's stated assumptions ([plant-results.md](plant-results.md)). **No evidence credit** (DQ-139). Asked for by fmdtools SG-02 (TE-02 step P2, MCS-02.7).

Straight road, 3.7 m and 3.0 m lanes, loss at t = 6 s, 8 s run. Times are seconds from the loss. The driver notices a drift of 0.2 m and corrects 1.5 s later (`driver_lat_cue_m`, `driver_t_r_s`). Each row spans 80, 95, 110 km/h.

**Assumption: the lateral push.** On a straight the plant drifts only when something pushes the car sideways. The push is a steady lateral acceleration standing in for a road crown, bank or crosswind (TC-15; WP-V-03 KS-02 names banks of 2–6 %): g × cross slope, so 0.2 m/s² is about a 2 % crown and 0.6 m/s² a 6 % bank. It builds up over 2–4 s, while the item still holds the lane, and stays on after the loss. Against the push the item holds the car off the lane centre (offset at the loss); the driver notices a drift of 0.2 m from that offset, while the lane line is reached from the offset, so with a strong push the line can come first.

**Caveat: fidelity L1.** The push is added as the driver's input is: no tyre self-aligning torque and no hands on the wheel resist it, so the drift here is an upper bound on what a real car does with the same push. Real values come from the EPS and vehicle measurements (WP-S-04 M-02) and the driver study (WP-C-08).

**What this shows** (generated from the rows above):

- With no push the car keeps its lane (peak drift 0.00 m): the model has nothing to drift it, which is an assumption about the road, not a property of the item.
- With any push from 0.1 m/s² the body reaches the 3.0 m lane line before the driver's correction starts in 24 of 24 runs.
- At about a 2 % crown (0.2 m/s²) the 3.0 m lane line is reached at 2.23–2.38 s, the correction starts at 3.08–3.23 s.
- The two stand-ins agree to within 0.03 s on the time to the 3.0 m lane line; speed barely matters, because a constant push drifts the car by time, not by distance.
- So the straight case is benign only if nothing pushes the car sideways at all; the finding for MCS-02.7 stands (forwarded to the FTA owner).
