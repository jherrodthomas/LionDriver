## What this shows (generated)

- Water outside is the common case: dew for about 680 hours of a normal year, most on humid summer and autumn nights, which the wipers clear, and frost for about 71, in winter. Frost is cleared before the drive, and **the camera's view clears last**: the defroster jet enters at the bottom, and the top of the glass clears about 2 min after half of it on a humid night.
- The normal year understates frost: real nights scatter around the normal, and the frosty ones are the clear, calm, colder ones. The count says when to expect it, not how often; local logs and photos from the car set it.
- Driving at 110 km/h, the outside air keeps the glass close to its own temperature; with fresh air the cabin stays dry enough (0 h a year alone, 0 h with four people).
- **Recirculation without air conditioning** is the case that fogs the glass while driving: within the hour in 3352 hours of a normal year, from October to May, and the count rests on how leaky the body is (65–4848 h). Automatic climate control dries the air and keeps it to 0 h.
- For KS-16 (camera obstruction): fog on the inside is a slow, whole-view obstruction that the driver also sees. Two lines for the user information and the installation checks: start only once the glass in front of the camera is clear, and keep the ventilation on fresh air or automatic in the cold months.
