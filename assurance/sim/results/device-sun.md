## What this shows (generated)

- **The windshield side is never shaded by the car.** The face sits just under the glass, so every direction in front of it and above the horizon is windshield; only the A-pillars cut the sun at grazing angles. What limits its sun is its angle: upright, it faces the sun squarely only when the sun is low ahead, and then through glass at a steep angle. Its most is 305 W/m², against the model's 320 W/m², on a winter day with the sun low ahead.
- **The screen side takes sun from behind**, through the rear window and, on the mesh, the roof panel: up to 1.22 W (0.96 W with a metal roof) when the sun is low behind the car. The windshield side is dark then, so the box's total stays under its peak; the heat model puts no sun on that face, and puts it into the box all the same.
- **The most sun into the box** in the year is 2.32 W with a glass roof panel and 2.30 W with a metal roof: 1.01 times the model's 2.30 W (inside its band of 1.61–3.00 W). It comes in winter, when the cabin is cool.
- **In June–August**, when the heat matters, it is at most 1.89 W: 0.82 times the model's. The high summer sun meets the upright face at a grazing angle.
- For the heat model (TE-04 T1): its constant is about the year's peak, and 1.2 times the most a summer day gives, so the hot-day bound carries margin on the sun. The model's input stays as it is until T1 measures the mount; then the summer value can replace it.
