## What this shows (generated)

- **The lamps themselves do not move the exposure.** At their closest in frame they cover at most 0.210 % of the exposure rectangle; the loop takes the median, so the road stays exposed and the lamps appear as small saturated spots.
- **Low beams across a median (inside the ODD)**: the veil stays under 3.4 % of the road even with a dirty windshield; the lamps leave the frame 21–82 m before the cars pass, so the closest, brightest part of the encounter is outside the camera's view.
- **An undipped high beam** is the case that matters. Across an 8 m median with 10 % scatter the veil leaves 20 % of the road's contrast and the loop darkens the road by up to 1.2 stops; on an undivided road (outside the ODD) 6 % and 1.9 stops. These take the beam's peak intensity in every direction, an upper bound: 20° off its axis a driving beam is much dimmer.
- For KS-12: the night test should include oncoming undipped high beams across the median and a dirty windshield, the two things that move the result. A measurement of this camera's veiling glare would turn the band into a line.
