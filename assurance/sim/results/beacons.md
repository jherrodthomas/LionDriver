## What this shows (generated)

- **The beacons hardly move the exposure until the last 50 m.** With a 2,000 cd beacon and 3 % scatter the road is darkened by at most 0.2 stops; at 50–100 m the swing is 0.01 stops. A beacon is far dimmer than a high beam: an undipped one across a median darkens the road by up to 1.2 stops (the headlight study).
- **Close in, each flash darkens the road.** openpilot's exposure reacts within a frame or two, and the last 50 m take 1.6 s, one to a few flashes: up to 0.6 stops in the worst case (3,100 cd at 1 Hz, a dirty windshield), with 0 frames darkened by more than a stop. Faster flashing is smoothed a little by the loop's averaging (0.6 stops at 1 Hz, 0.4 at 4 Hz).
- **Flashing that stays partly on** (10 % between flashes, as Rea and Bullough recommend) hardly changes it: the flashes' peaks set the veil.
- **Passing**: lane centring leaves 0.94–1.94 m to the vehicle and 0.34–1.34 m to a person at its door. Move-over laws ask the driver to change lanes or slow down; the item does neither, so it stays the driver's task (TC-36).
- For KS tests at night: a vehicle on the shoulder with flashing beacons, a dirty windshield, and a check of the lane lines while the image blinks in the last 50 m. A log of this camera's veiling glare would turn the band into a line.
