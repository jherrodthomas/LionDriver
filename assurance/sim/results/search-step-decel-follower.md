The request steps to the envelope minimum, −3.5 m/s² (F-02, L-03: the panda passes it, the onset limit TSR-205 is not implemented), or to −6.0 m/s² (L-04: refused), with a vehicle following at `follow_gap` seconds. The ego driver, cued by the deceleration, overrides with the accelerator after the reaction time; the following driver brakes at `f_brake` after `f_t_r` from the ego's brake lights (on above 1 m/s²), until no faster than the ego.

Faults: L-03 deceleration step −3.5 m/s², L-04 deceleration −6.0 m/s². Robustness: rho = the smallest bumper gap to the following vehicle after the fault; rho < 0 means contact. Past contact the plant lets the vehicles overlap, so a negative magnitude means little; the sign, the time to contact and the closing speed at contact are what count.

What the plant cannot show: it has **no tyre friction limit** (the driver's braking of up to 8 m/s² and the lead's are taken as given on any road; on a wet road (W02) about 5 m/s² is what the tyres give, so a row that needs more is worse there) and **no perception** (the ACC sees its lead at once, exactly and at any range, so a lead detected late, a stopped vehicle not taken as a lead (SH-06) or a cut-in is not searched; low visibility acts only through the driver). The ACC is a model, not openpilot's MPC; the lead brakes at a constant rate; the following driver brakes after a reaction time to the ego's brake lights (on above 1 m/s²) at a set deceleration until no faster than the ego, and does not steer round it. The driver is a model with stated cues and reactions; the PCM response is assumed (M-03).
