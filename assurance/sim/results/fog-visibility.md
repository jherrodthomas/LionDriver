# Fog: how far the road camera sees

> **Synthetic data (DSR-12). Design aid, no evidence credit (DQ-413).** Generated by [`weather/fog_visibility.py`](../weather/fog_visibility.py); do not edit by hand. Koschmieder's law; the contrast openpilot's model needs is not known, so it is a band (2–5 %).

Visibility is the meteorological optical range (WMO, 5 % contrast). A stopped car is seen against the fog-lit horizon (inherent contrast 0.6–1.0); the lane lines against the road (contrast 0.6, NCHRP 20-102(06)), where the fog's airlight is 5–12 times the road's own luminance.

## What this shows (generated)

- **For the item to stop for a stopped car** at −3.5 m/s² (149 m), the camera has to see it that far: visibility of at least 114–179 m. An unwarned driver stopping on a wet road (188 m) needs 144–227 m.
- **The lane lines go first.** Seen against the road through the fog's airlight, they fade much sooner than a car against the sky: a 61 m preview (2 s at 110 km/h) needs a visibility of 96–281 m.
- **Against the ODD's bound of 200 m** (WP-C-02): the item's own stop needs at most 179 m of visibility, inside the bound. The lines need 212 m with the nominal inputs (5 % threshold, airlight 8 times the road) and up to 281 m at the cautious end: the 200 m bound is about right nominally and short at the cautious end. The camera's real contrast threshold, from local logs in fog, decides whether it needs to move; until then this is a margin to watch, not a reason to change it.
- Not covered: truck spray (no quantitative source), the road camera's own exposure in fog, and the model's real contrast threshold, which local logs in fog would show.
