The ego follows a lead with its ACC at the gap `gap_s`; at 6 s the lead brakes at `lead_decel` to a stop, and `dt_fault` later the item's deceleration is lost: silently (a stale plan holds zero acceleration, F-02 M01; the frames go on and the panda passes them) or by a SoC stall (F-04b: the panda revokes and sounds its siren, the PCM takes the missing frames as zero). The driver is cued by the missing deceleration (an acceleration 1 m/s² away from what a working ACC would give), the siren or the closing (`ttc_cue`), and brakes at `brake` after the reaction time, at least as hard as the car already does. A lead braking harder than the envelope's −3.5 m/s² can be too much for the ACC alone: the run without the fault says so.

Faults: F-04b SoC stall, deceleration silently lost (F-02 M01). Robustness: rho = the smallest bumper gap to the lead after the fault; rho < 0 means contact. Past contact the plant lets the vehicles overlap, so a negative magnitude means little; the sign, the time to contact and the closing speed at contact are what count.

What the plant cannot show: it has **no tyre friction limit** (the driver's braking of up to 8 m/s² and the lead's are taken as given on any road; on a wet road (W02) about 5 m/s² is what the tyres give, so a row that needs more is worse there) and **no perception** (the ACC sees its lead at once, exactly and at any range, so a lead detected late, a stopped vehicle not taken as a lead (SH-06) or a cut-in is not searched; low visibility acts only through the driver). The ACC is a model, not openpilot's MPC; the lead brakes at a constant rate; the following driver brakes after a reaction time to the ego's brake lights (on above 1 m/s²) at a set deceleration until no faster than the ego, and does not steer round it. The driver is a model with stated cues and reactions; the PCM response is assumed (M-03).
