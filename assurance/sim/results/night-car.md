## What this shows (generated)

- **At 149 m, where the item would have to start braking from 110 km/h, the car's rear is above the cut-off and gets 0.032–0.158 lux** from our low beams (at most). A dark rear is then 9–12 stops under the road the exposure is set on, 1.2 of 255 at best: black. A white rear is 5–7 stops under.
- **There is little silhouette either.** A dark rear and the road beside it are both under 0.001 cd/m²: the rear is -30 % against a dry road and +377 % against a wet one, which returns even less of the light. Both are lit by the same beam, so the paint's colour, not the light, sets the difference, and in the image both are near black.
- **The reflectors are the brightest part of an unlit car.** At the regulation's minimum each is 5.2 cd/m², 2 stops over the road the exposure is set on, but 1.0 px across in the road camera, 0.3 px in the model's input: two red dots.
- **A driving beam changes it**: at least 32,400 cd straight ahead puts 2.9 lux on the rear at 149 m, and a dark rear is 5 stops under the road's exposure, a white one at about the road's level (39 of 255). Highway traffic usually means low beams.
- **Over the ODD's speed** the item needs 175 m from 120 km/h (TC-20), further into the dark.
- So at night on an unlit road the model would have to take an unlit stopped car from its reflectors and its silhouette against whatever lies beyond, not from the light on it. A lit car (tail or hazard lights) is a different case: its lamps are far brighter than anything here.
