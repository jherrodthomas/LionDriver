# Search: A fault in a curve at R_ODD at a speed near the ODD bound

The curve has the radius R_ODD = (110 km/h)² / 2.5 m/s² = 373 m; at 100–110 km/h its lateral acceleration is 2.07–2.5 m/s², above the 1.99 m/s² the item's full torque gives (plant case N-3), so the car drifts outward even without a fault. Five faults, including the hard-over mirrored to the left (toward the inside of the curve), at the curve entry, on lanes of 3.0–3.9 m. Each catalogue row is also run without the fault, to separate what the fault adds from what the curve does anyway.

Faults: F-01b hard-over (right), F-01b mirrored (left), F-03 frozen command, F-04b SoC stall, F-07 EPS fault. Robustness: rho = (lane width − 1.781 m) / 2 − max |lateral offset| after the fault; rho < 0 means the body edge crossed a lane line. Beyond about −2 m the car is in the next lane and the magnitude means little; the sign and the time to the line are what count.

What the plant cannot show: it has **no tyre friction limit** (a linear tyre: a wet or icy road, a braking driver in a curve and a correction of 4 m/s² all behave as on a dry road, so nothing here says whether the car would slide) and **no perception** (the lane-keeping controller sees its lane exactly, so no perception insufficiency, wrong line or late detection is searched; the faults are injected at the actuator side). The driver is a model with stated reactions, the EPS response to each fault is assumed (M-01, M-02), and road users beside the ego are not modelled: the time to the lane line is how soon an adjacent vehicle or barrier would be reached, not whether it is hit.
