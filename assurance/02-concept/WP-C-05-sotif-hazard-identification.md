## 1. Purpose and scope

This document identifies hazards that the **intended functionality** of LD-SDA can cause without any E/E fault: through insufficiencies of the specification, performance limits of perception, ML and planning, and reasonably foreseeable misuse. It evaluates their risk with the HARA method, links them to the functional-safety hazard log, and defines the acceptance criteria that later validation ([WP-V-02](../06-validation/WP-V-02-sotif-vv-strategy.md)) must meet.

Scope: reference configuration ([WP-C-01](WP-C-01-item-definition.md)) inside the reference ODD ([WP-C-02](WP-C-02-odd-and-intended-functionality.md)). Experimental Mode is analysed (SH-09) because the toggle stays available, although its default is off since 2026-10-14 (D-08, DQ-001) and WP-C-02 §6.4 recommends excluding it.

Since 2026-10-14 the ODD is **highways only** (decision register DQ-023 option B): the arterial and rural part ODD-A is no longer claimed. WP-C-02 is not yet updated (its owner's part of DQ-023). Hazards whose example situations lie on arterial or rural roads (SH-09, SH-11, the head-on part of SH-01/SH-02) are kept, because the item can still be engaged there: they are reached through an ODD exit (SH-13, misuse MC-07) and are no longer used to set validation targets.

Since 2026-10-19 the ODD claim stays highways only, and every off-highway environment seen in upstream use is analysed as foreseeable misuse (decision register DQ-213 option A, review RR-2026-011). WP-C-03 v0.6 rates engagement there at 1–10 % of engaged time (E3; roundabouts and road works E2) and adds OS-19 roundabout, OS-20 road works and OS-21 road without markings. Here they are example situations of SH-01, SH-02, SH-11 and SH-13. They set no validation target (§5).

Inputs: [WP-C-03 HARA](WP-C-03-hara.md) (H-01…H-06, H-08; OS-01…OS-11, OS-19…OS-21), `docs/LIMITATIONS.md`, gap assessment GAP-16…GAP-23, onboard code review.

## 2. Method

1. **Hazardous behaviour identification.** For each function F-01…F-07, ask: what output does the intended functionality produce that is hazardous in some situation of the ODD, while every component works as designed? Sources: `docs/LIMITATIONS.md`, known L2 field experience (WP-C-01 §6), the code review, and the misuse analysis ([WP-C-08](WP-C-08-driver-hmi-misuse-analysis.md)).
2. **Numbering.** SH-0x matches H-0x where the vehicle-level effect is the same (hazard log convention, [WP-C-03 §7](WP-C-03-hara.md#7-hazard-log-cross-references)). H-07 (mode confusion) is a malfunction hazard in the HARA (since WP-C-03 v0.2) with no separate SOTIF entry, so SH-07 is not used. SOTIF-only hazards start at SH-09; the HARA's Experimental Mode variant hazard H-10 (not stopping for a signal or sign; H-09 until WP-C-03 v0.3) has the same vehicle-level effect as SH-09. Since WP-C-03 v0.4, H-09 is the unresponsive-driver hazard (driver monitoring failing while the driver does not supervise), the malfunction counterpart of SH-12.
3. **Severity and controllability.** Reused from the HARA hazardous event with the same effect and situation. Where no HARA event exists, rated here with the HARA scales and marked "new".
4. **Exposure** is not used to compute an ASIL (ISO 21448 does not assign ASILs). It is recorded to help set validation targets.
5. **Evaluation.** A hazard with S0 or C0 needs no further SOTIF treatment. Every other hazard goes into the triggering-condition analysis ([WP-C-06](WP-C-06-sotif-insufficiencies-triggering-conditions.md)) and needs an acceptance criterion (§5).
6. **Shared with the fault trees.** Some performance limits are also branches of a functional-safety goal's fault tree ([WP-A-04](../08-analyses/WP-A-04-system-fta-fmea.md)), because the goal names the hazardous effect whatever its cause:
   - SH-04 under SG-04 (false braking).
   - SH-02 under SG-02 (G2.8, a curve too tight for the torque limit). Decided 2026-10-18 by the project lead, decision register DQ-361 option A.
   - SH-06 under SG-06 (G6.9, a lead missed or seen late). Same decision.

   The SOTIF analysis evaluates the rate and controllability of these limits (WP-C-06 §5.1, VT-02, VT-04, VT-06). The fault trees carry them as basic events marked [SOTIF] and show that the warning path depends on the same SoC.

## 5. Acceptance criteria

### 5.1 Approach

The top-level claim ([WP-M-01 §3.2](../01-management/WP-M-01-assurance-strategy.md#32-top-level-claim)) is that residual risk is **no worse than manual driving of the same vehicle with its stock TSS 2.0 assistance**. The acceptance criteria follow that claim in two layers:

1. **Quantitative residual-risk criterion (per SH group).** The rate of harm caused by SH-xx while engaged shall not exceed the corresponding rate for human drivers in comparable conditions (same road types, US), reduced by a factor that reflects that the stock vehicle with LKA/ACC is the comparison baseline. The specific numbers are **deferred to [WP-V-02](../06-validation/WP-V-02-sotif-vv-strategy.md)** (validation targets VT-nn), derived as in §5.2.
2. **Qualitative criteria** that do not need statistics and are checked by analysis and test (§5.3).

### 5.2 Derivation method for validation targets (to be applied in WP-V-02)

1. **Baseline rate.** Take the human-driver crash rate A_H for the relevant severity (e.g. injury crashes, fatal crashes) and road class from US public data (NHTSA FARS / CRSS, FHWA VMT). Record source, year and road class. Use the road-class-specific rate (Interstate vs arterial), not the national average. *Decided 2026-10-25 (decision register DQ-137 option A):* NHTSA FARS and CRSS crashes over FHWA travel data, split Interstate against other arterials. Source, year, filter and known limits are recorded in [WP-V-02 §2.1](../06-validation/WP-V-02-sotif-vv-strategy.md#21-b1-human-driver-data-set-dq-137). No rate has been extracted yet (WP-V-02 OI-2).
2. **Stock TSS2 baseline.** Where data allow, adjust A_H for vehicles with LKA/ACC/PCS (published insurance or NHTSA studies). If no adequate data exist, use A_H and state the assumption. *Since DQ-137 A:* A_H is not adjusted. The data do not say whether the human car had LKA or ACC, and the adjustment (option B) was not chosen. The assumption stated is that A_H is human driving in the mixed US fleet. The stock-TSS2 comparison is made per scenario by paired closed-course runs instead (B2, decision register DQ-138 option A, [WP-V-02 §2.2](../06-validation/WP-V-02-sotif-vv-strategy.md#22-b2-paired-runs-against-stock-tss2-dq-138)). Whether the rate criterion of §5.1 item 1 carries a fixed factor is settled with the target freeze (WP-V-02 OI-3).
3. **Allocation.** Split the acceptable rate across SH groups (lateral SH-01/02/10, longitudinal SH-03/04/06/11, mode/override SH-05/09, SH-08) in proportion to their share of the baseline crash types. Record the split.
4. **Controllability credit.** Not every hazardous behaviour leads to harm: the driver controls most. The share of uncontrolled events is estimated from controllability tests ([WP-C-08 §8](WP-C-08-driver-hmi-misuse-analysis.md)). Using it lets the target be expressed as a rate of **hazardous behaviours** (e.g. lane exits, unexpected decelerations > x m/s²) per km, which is measurable.
5. **Target distance.** For a zero-event demonstration at confidence 1 − α, the required exposure is d = −ln(α) / λ (≈ 3/λ for 95 %). With observed events, use the chi-square bound. The required distance is per ODD part; since DQ-023 that is ODD-H only.
6. **Evidence mix.** Distance can be split between fleet/replay data (driving-model replay on fork-owned logs), simulation and on-road validation, with the weighting justified in WP-V-02.
