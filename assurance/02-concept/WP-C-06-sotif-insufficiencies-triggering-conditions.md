## 1. Purpose and method

This document lists the functional insufficiencies (FI-nn) of LD-SDA and the triggering conditions (TC-nn) that can activate them, links each pair to the SOTIF hazards of [WP-C-05](WP-C-05-sotif-hazard-identification.md), and evaluates whether the system's response is acceptable.

- **Specification insufficiency (S):** the specified behaviour itself is hazardous in some situation (e.g. soft disable keeps actuating on failed inputs).
- **Performance insufficiency (P):** the specification is fine, but a component does not perform well enough in some conditions (e.g. lane perception in glare).
- **Sources:** `docs/LIMITATIONS.md`, the onboard code review, the gap assessment (GAP-16…GAP-23), and domain knowledge of camera/radar L2 systems.
- **Evaluation outcome:** `Acceptable` (risk is low and controllable; user information suffices), `Needs modification` (a functional modification in [WP-C-07](WP-C-07-sotif-functional-modifications.md) or an ODD restriction is required), `Needs validation` (acceptability depends on evidence from [WP-V-03](../06-validation/WP-V-03-sotif-known-scenarios.md)).

The driving model is a black box from LionDriver's point of view: training data and its ODD are not available (GAP-22). Its insufficiencies are therefore stated at the level of observable outputs.

### 5.2 Off-highway observations (RR-2026-011)

The ODD audit RR-2026-011 (PR #290) checked an owner's video of openpilot on the reference Corolla driving back roads. Two of its observations concern this document:

- **A-5 → TC-35.** In a curve the lead was not seen while the car accelerated to the set speed, then it was detected late and the car braked hard. Curves under acceleration also occur on highway ramps and interchanges, so TC-35 is a triggering condition inside ODD-H, evaluated with the other lead-detection rows.
- **A-6, centre-line crossing.** On narrow roads the car crossed the double yellow line and cut corners. This is intended-function behaviour of the driving model (FI-01, FI-03) leading to SH-01. The roads where it happened are outside ODD-H (highways only, DQ-023), so no triggering condition is added for them: the ODD exclusion, FM-08 and user information treat it. The lead decided DQ-213 on 2026-10-19 (option A): the ODD claim stays highways only, and WP-C-03 v0.6 rates engagement on such roads as foreseeable misuse at 1–10 % of engaged time (E3, OS-03 and OS-21). The audit found that the 60 km/h engagement floor proposed for FM-08 would not stop a 72 km/h back road (its F-2/F-5). If the ODD is ever extended to rural roads, A-6 becomes a triggering condition here and needs a known scenario.

### 5.1 Performance limits in the SG-02 and SG-06 fault trees

Two performance limits also appear as branches of the functional-safety fault trees ([WP-A-04](../08-analyses/WP-A-04-system-fta-fmea.md)):

- **G2.8**, a curve too tight for the steering torque limit, under SG-02: TC-14, FI-09 and FI-11, leading to SH-02.
- **G6.9**, a lead missed or seen too late, under SG-06: TC-08, TC-09, TC-17 and TC-33, through FI-04, FI-06 and FI-10, leading to SH-06.

The project lead decided on 2026-10-18 that SG-02 and SG-06 keep these branches (decision register DQ-361, option A), as SG-04 keeps false braking.

The split of work is as follows:

- **Fault trees.** They show that the take-over warning has no independent path. The warning comes from the same SoC that fell short.
- **SOTIF, this document and WP-V-02.** It evaluates how often the limits occur and whether they are controllable: VT-02 and VT-06, with the rows above.
- **The SOTIF basic events** BE-2.23 and BE-6.09 take their rates from that evaluation. Neither analysis credits the other with a result it has not produced.
