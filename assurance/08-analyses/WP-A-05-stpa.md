## 1. Purpose and scope

The HARA ([WP-C-03](../02-concept/WP-C-03-hara.md)), the FTA/FMEA ([WP-A-04](WP-A-04-system-fta-fmea.md), [WP-W-04](../05-software/WP-W-04-software-safety-analysis.md), [`fmea/`](fmea/fmea-plan.md)) and the DFA ([WP-A-03](WP-A-03-dependent-failure-analysis.md)) start from component failures. STPA starts from the control structure. It finds hazards that arise when every component works as specified but the control actions are unsafe in context: wrong process models (the driver believes the item is off; openpilot believes the driver is attentive), missing or late feedback, conflicting controllers (driver, openpilot, EPS, PCM, stock ADAS), and unsafe interactions between modes.

Scope: the reference configuration CFG-001 (Corolla E210 · ZRE212 · US: US sedan, model years 2020–2022, 1.8 L CVT, TSS 2.0; comma 3X with integrated panda, openpilot longitudinal, Chill Mode per D-08) while driving on public roads. The vehicle ECUs (EPS, PCM, brake ECU, stock ADAS camera, instrument cluster) are in the control structure as controllers outside the item; their unsafe control actions are recorded as assumptions, not analysed.

Method: STPA is the hazard-analysis method of STAMP, the systems-theoretic accident model (Leveson). This document is the item's STAMP analysis; step 4 classifies causal factors by the STPA Handbook's control-loop categories (Figure 5-1). Field incidents are analysed with the STAMP incident method, CAST, in the field-monitoring process (proposed, see OI-16).

Not in scope here: cybersecurity attackers (TARA, [WP-C-09](../02-concept/WP-C-09-tara.md)), installation and service processes (PFMEA), Experimental Mode (excluded from claims, D-08).

## 2. Step 1 — Purpose of the analysis

### 2.1 Losses

| ID | Loss |
|---|---|
| L-1 | Death or injury of vehicle occupants |
| L-2 | Death or injury of other road users (including vulnerable road users) |
| L-3 | Damage to the vehicle or to property outside it |
| L-4 | Loss of the stock vehicle safety functions the owner relies on (PCS/AEB, LTA/LDA) |

L-4 is a loss in its own right because the owner did not consent to losing them by installing the item, and it leads to L-1…L-3.

### 2.2 System-level hazards

The functional-safety hazards of the HARA are used as the system-level hazards, so that STPA results trace to the same safety goals. STPA adds one hazard that the HARA rates inside other hazards.

| ID | System-level hazard | Losses | Safety goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| H-01 | Unintended or excessive lateral motion | L-1, L-2, L-3 | SG-01 |
| H-02 | Unannounced loss or degradation of lateral control | L-1, L-2, L-3 | SG-02 |
| H-03 | Unintended acceleration | L-1, L-2, L-3 | SG-03 |
| H-04 | Unintended or excessive deceleration | L-1, L-2, L-3 | SG-04 |
| H-05 | Driver unable to override or disengage | L-1, L-2, L-3 | SG-05 |
| H-06 | Unannounced loss of longitudinal deceleration | L-1, L-2, L-3 | SG-06 |
| H-07 | Displayed system state differs from actual state (mode confusion) | L-1, L-2, L-3 | (rated within H-02, H-05, H-06) |
| H-08 | Suppression of the vehicle's stock pre-collision braking | L-4, L-1, L-2 | SG-07 |
| H-STPA-1 | The vehicle is driven with the item engaged while the driver is not supervising it | L-1, L-2, L-3 | H-09 (WP-C-03 v0.4, situations OS-17/OS-18, SG-08 ASIL A): foreseeable misuse in both SOTIF and the HARA, decided 2026-10-14 ([WP-C-08](../02-concept/WP-C-08-driver-hmi-misuse-analysis.md) §6.1, decision register DQ-003, DQ-205) |

### 2.3 System-level constraints

| ID | Constraint | Hazard |
|---|---|---|
| SC-1 | Lateral motion caused by the item must stay within what the driver can control, and must be zero while the item is not engaged | H-01 |
| SC-2 | Any loss or degradation of lateral or longitudinal control while engaged must be announced to the driver in time to take over | H-02, H-06 |
| SC-3 | Positive acceleration must be commanded only while engaged and must stay within the controllable envelope | H-03 |
| SC-4 | Deceleration must stay within what the driver and following traffic can control | H-04 |
| SC-5 | The driver must be able to override or disengage at any time by braking, cancelling, steering or accelerating, and the item must yield at once | H-05 |
| SC-6 | The state the driver perceives (engaged, overriding, disengaged, Chill/Experimental) must match the actual state | H-07 |
| SC-7 | The stock PCS/AEB function must remain available and unaltered whether the item is engaged or not | H-08 |
| SC-8 | The item must be engaged only while the driver supervises it, and must reach a minimal-risk behaviour when the driver stops supervising (v0.5: for an unresponsive driver this becomes the H-09 ASIL A goal, DQ-205) | H-STPA-1 |

### 3.2 Controllers, responsibilities and process models

| ID | Controller | Responsibilities (relevant to the losses) | Process-model variables that matter |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Driver | Supervise at all times; engage only in suitable conditions; override or disengage when the item misbehaves; respond to alerts | Item engaged / overriding / disengaged; lateral vs longitudinal authority; Chill vs Experimental; what the item can handle (curves, lights, stopped vehicles); whether stock AEB is available |
| C2 | openpilot (SoC) | Plan and command lateral torque and acceleration within the envelope; detect faults and driver inattention; alert; disengage on driver input | Driver attentive; driver overriding; vehicle state (speed, cruise, brake, gas); own health; panda state; model confidence |
| C3 | panda safety model | Pass only commands within limits and only while authority is granted; withdraw authority on brake, cruise off, stale data, lost SoC; keep stock PCS path | controls_allowed; cruise engaged; brake/gas pressed; message freshness; SoC alive; intercept drive *as commanded* (the actual switch position is not fed back, §3.5); safety mode |
| C4 | EPS ECU (external) | Apply driver assist; apply LKA torque within its own limits; fault out LKA on implausible requests | LKA request valid; driver torque |
| C5 | PCM / ACC / brake ECU (external) | Hold cruise state; execute acceleration requests within its own limits; cancel cruise on brake | Cruise engaged; brake pedal; requested acceleration |
| C6 | Stock ADAS camera (external) | Detect collision risk; command PCS braking and warnings | Obstacles; own state (it keeps running while its LKA/ACC messages are blocked) |

### 3.5 Harness box: the intercept element (v0.4, RR-2026-010)

Up to v0.3 the analysis treated the intercept element as an electromechanical relay. RR-2026-010 read comma.ai's published v3 harness-box drawing: there is no relay. The element that executes CA-09 is:

| Property | Published v3 drawing (RR-2026-010 §2) | What it changes in the control structure |
|---|---|---|
| Actuator | Two DG419LE analog SPDT switches (U1 on CAN_H, U2 on CAN_L), both inputs on SBU1 (HB-01) | CA-09 ends at two powered ICs, not a coil. | Default | SBU1 pulled to GND through R2 + R3 (2 × 120 Ω); undriven means pass-through, the camera's CAN joined to the car's (HB-02) | Release after a reset, watchdog or hang relies on the pull-down. If R2 or R3 is open, the inputs float and the switches may stay in intercept with the MCU silent (DFM-010.C3, F-03) → LS-33 |
| Supply | V+ and VL on the box supply VIN: ignition through D1, or comma power (HB-03); Schottky clamps D2/D3 from both buses to VIN and GND (HB-04) | The stock camera path (CA-13) passes active parts that need power **in pass-through too**. | Feedback | None (F-06) | C3's process model holds the commanded drive only. No controller in the item can see that CA-13 no longer reaches C5 → UCA-36 |

HWSR-702 ("no active component in the stock path") cannot be met with this box (F-01). Whether to accept and test it, or build a LionDriver adapter with a passive fail-safe relay and a readback contact, is DQ-209, queued for the lead. ## 5. Step 4 — Loss scenarios

v0.1 wrote scenarios for the priority UCAs only. v0.3 re-checks those against the current code and adds a scenario for every other UCA of §4.1–§4.3 and §4.6. The UCAs of external controllers (§4.4) rest on assumptions of use, except UCA-42, whose behaviour has a scenario because the item causes it.

**Code basis (v0.3):** `liondriver-dev` `26104ab`, panda `a55bdfc`, opendbc `a57b667`. Since BL-003 the reference build has become the default build (`SConstruct:274-283`). Its features are therefore credited here, and the status records which scenarios they change.

**Code basis (v0.4):** `liondriver-dev` `1033d98`, panda `a55bdfc` (unchanged), opendbc `9a13f60`. Every code reference below was re-read at this commit and corrected where lines moved. Changes since v0.3 that the scenarios now credit: the PCM-edge heartbeat gate (opendbc `9a13f60`, TSR-301) and the host's 0.3 s engagement grace for `controlsMismatch` (TSR-621); TSR-615 immediate disables on heartbeat loss, TX-rate revocation and a rising `safetyTxBlocked` count (`supervision.py`, `selfdrived.py:368-375`; in the code since #51, not credited in v0.3); the assured-configuration check that logs, but does not show or block, platform, parameter and software-origin deviations (`assured_config.py`, CA-008); the relaxed following-distance default (CA-011); the stock-AEB interlock on alpha longitudinal and ECU knockout (`stock_aeb_interlock.py`, CA-001); Chill mode by default (DQ-001). The pending opendbc changes ATC-20 and ATC-26 (re-grant only after a full TX-rate window and after a new engagement) are not pinned and not credited. The harness box is re-based on RR-2026-010 (§3.5). None of this is verified on the bench or the vehicle.

**Status:**
- *covered*: a requirement, mechanism or analysis row exists and the scenario is controlled, or will be once the requirement is implemented.
- *known gap*: another analysis already records the gap.
- *new*: no other analysis records it (§6).
- *open test*: it can only be settled on the vehicle or the bench.

Every scenario is linked to fault-tree events of [WP-A-04](WP-A-04-system-fta-fmea.md), FMEA failure modes and TSRs in [stpa-map.yaml](stpa-map.yaml). `tools/safety/fmea_lint.py` checks that every UCA has a scenario, that every link exists, and that each cited event lies in the fault tree of the scenario's hazard.

### 5.8 Scenario cards (v0.14)

One card per loss scenario of §5.1–§5.5, on four sheets by the controller of its first UCA (C1; C2; C2.x and C7; C3 to C6). Each card tells the scenario in three steps: the cause, what the controller then believes or never learns (the step is named after the scenario's causal-factor type: *Feedback*, *Belief*, *Algorithm*, *Effect* or *Then*), and the unsafe control action. Below it are a road scene, the Covered-by IDs of its row, and its status and open reason from [stpa-map.yaml](stpa-map.yaml). A covered scenario with a residual (a known gap, an open test, or no vehicle verification) shows that residual from its Status cell, and the build fails if a covered row names one the card leaves out. The steps are short paraphrases. Each quotes a phrase of its row (or of its UCA), and the build fails if that phrase changes, so a changed row needs its card redrawn. The scenes are schematic.
