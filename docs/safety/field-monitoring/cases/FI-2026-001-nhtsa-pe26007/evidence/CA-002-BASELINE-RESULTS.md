## What the harness covers, and what it doesn't

The harness runs the **real** radard lead selection (`RadarD`) and the **real** `LongitudinalPlanner` / MPC in closed loop, for the reference vehicle (`TOYOTA_COROLLA_TSS2`), with its longitudinal actuator delay.

Simplifications. Each one makes the results **optimistic**:

| Simplification | Effect | Covered later by |
|---|---|---|
| Perception is idealized: once the target is in range, vision confirms it at once (lead prob 1.0), with no noise or dropouts | Real detection latency and flicker add distance | CA-003 (recall vs range), phase 2 with logged model outputs |
| Commanded acceleration is tracked perfectly after the actuator delay. The longitudinal PID, brake ramp, road grade and friction are not modeled | Real cars reach -3.5 m/s² later | HIL / closed-course phase |
| No driver, no stock AEB | Shows openpilot alone (barrier L1 in the RCA) | — (deliberate) |
| Straight road; target fully in lane (lateral offset 0) | Partly-in-lane targets (TC-06), curves and crests not covered | Phase 2 (simulator with camera rendering) |
| Day/night, glare and target appearance not represented | Needs a perception model | CA-003, phase 2 |

## Findings

1. **The planner itself is not the bottleneck.** Once the target is confirmed, the MPC goes to the -3.5 m/s² limit almost at once. The simulated minimum detection range is about 92–95% of the RCA §3.1 estimate. (The RCA assumed 0.5 s of latency; the harness has about 0.2 s.) At 120 km/h a stopped vehicle has to be confirmed at **≥ 166 m**. **Supports RC-02:** the limit comes from detection range combined with the braking cap.
2. **Radar adds nothing for stationary targets at speed.** The required range is identical with and without radar. A target that only radar sees is **ignored above 4 m/s**, and the car hits it at full speed, even at 40 km/h. **RC-02 / FTA B3 confirmed in SIL** (was "Verified design"). Kept as an `expectedFailure` test, so a fix shows up as a test change.
3. **Cut-out reveals inside the stopping distance end in high-speed impacts:** 47–102 km/h in 6 of 15 cases. In every collision the reveal gap was shorter than the stopping distance at -3.5 m/s², so these crashes cannot be avoided at the current braking authority. Example: 120 km/h, revealed at 120 m → impact at 64 km/h. At about -8 m/s² that case needs about 86 m, so it would be avoided. **Input to CA-004** (emergency braking-authority study).
4. **FCW fires in every collision case.** Where the reveal leaves ≥ 2.5 s, the FCW comes at ≥ 2.0 s TTC (2.78 s and 3.27 s). With idealized perception, AC-2 passes. In the field the FCW shares perception with braking (RC-03), so a late detection delays both. CA-005 still stands.
5. **AC-1 passes** at 40–120 km/h for stopped and slow targets confirmed at 1.2× the required distance. The braking-limit test (confirmed at exactly 1.0×) also passes.
